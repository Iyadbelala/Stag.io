\chapter{Preliminary Study and Requirements Specification}

\section{Introduction}

In the previous chapter, we presented the general context of the project and studied existing solutions in the field of internship management. In this second chapter, we will present the project to be developed, identify the different actors that interact with the system, and then establish the requirements specification by defining both functional and non-functional requirements. Finally, we will model the use cases along with their detailed textual descriptions.

\section{Project Presentation}

\subsection{Project Objective}

The \textbf{Stag.io} project (\textit{stage} is the French word for \textit{internship}) aims to design and develop a modern web platform dedicated to managing the complete internship lifecycle. The platform seeks to digitize the entire process --- from the publication of internship offers by companies, through student applications, to the administrative validation of internship agreements by universities.

The main objective is to provide a unified space where all stakeholders in the internship process (students, companies, universities, and administrators) can collaborate efficiently and transparently, replacing traditional methods that often rely on manual exchanges (emails, paper documents, in-person visits).

\subsection{Problem Statement}

As established in the previous chapter, the traditional internship management process suffers from several interrelated problems: the fragmentation of offers across disconnected channels, inefficient profile-to-offer matching, manual paper-based administrative workflows, lack of real-time visibility into application and agreement statuses, and the absence of structured post-internship feedback mechanisms. These problems collectively create a sub-optimal experience for all stakeholders and motivate the development of a unified digital solution.

\subsection{Proposed Solution}

To address these issues, we propose \textbf{Stag.io}, a full-stack web platform with the following key capabilities:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SmartMatch}: A proprietary recommendation engine that ranks offers by relevance to the student's profile across four dimensions: skills (50\%), department (25\%), location (15\%), and title match (10\%), producing scores in under 100\,ms.
    \item \textbf{Complete lifecycle management}: Offer publication, intelligent discovery, application submission, agreement validation, and post-internship reviews --- all within a single platform.
    \item \textbf{Real-time communication}: Instant notifications via WebSocket (Socket.IO) for application status changes, new applications, and validation requests.
    \item \textbf{AI chatbot}: An integrated conversational assistant powered by Google Gemini with conversation history.
    \item \textbf{Secure authentication}: JWT-based authentication (access + refresh tokens) with OTP email verification, CAPTCHA protection, and role-based access control across five roles.
    \item \textbf{Document generation}: Built-in CV and internship agreement generation in PDF format, with full bilingual support (English/French).
\end{itemize}

The requirements specification defines what the system must do (functional requirements) and the quality attributes it must satisfy (non-functional requirements). These requirements are derived from the project objectives, the actor analysis, and the limitations of existing solutions identified in Chapter~1.

\begin{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

In this chapter, we presented the Stag.io project in detail, identified its primary and secondary actors, and formalized the functional and non-functional requirements. We then modeled the main interactions through a use case diagram and detailed three essential use cases: authentication, internship application, and offer management. These elements establish the functional bridge between stakeholder needs and system behavior, and form the foundation for the design and modeling work presented in the next chapter.
